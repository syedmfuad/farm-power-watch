How these numbers are built

The figures track the realized agricultural electricity price (revenue ÷ electricity sold on farm and irrigation rate schedules in utilities' FERC Form 1 filings, through 2025) across 33 providers in 22 states, and relate its growth to the operating data center capacity each utility carries. Over 2022–2025, agricultural prices grew faster where more data center capacity was operating; at a representative 364 MW the estimate corresponds to about +12.5% faster price growth, and it scales with capacity from there. The per-farm dollar figures apply that estimate to average farm utilities spending (USDA Census of Agriculture 2022).

These estimates come from ongoing research linking data center growth to the electricity prices farms pay; the relationship is strongest where data center capacity is largest. scenario figures apply the same estimate to larger operating capacity: this utility's planned capacity (Cleanview; 25% realized by 2028, 50% by 2030), its state's trajectory, or LBNL/DOE national load-growth scenarios (176 TWh in 2023 → 325–580 TWh in 2028). They are illustrative, not forecasts.

Data: Cleanview data center census; FERC Form 1 (PUDL); USDA Census of Agriculture 2022; LBNL-2001637; HIFLD service territories. Source: Data Centers and Agricultural Electricity Prices (2026).
