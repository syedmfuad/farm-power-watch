Data Centers & Agriculture Observatory

Data centers compete with farms for three shared resources — electricity, land, and water. This site has one interactive tool for each. Use the menu above to open them; this page explains what each shows, how to use it, and what the numbers mean.

⚡ Electricity

Farm Power Watch. The data-center boom is driving up the electricity prices farmers pay. See how much faster farm power prices are climbing — and what it's costing a typical farm — by utility and county.

How to use it

  1. Search your utility or county in the search box.
  2. Read the cards for the estimated price effect and the cost to a typical farm.
  3. Open "Project forward" — drag the year slider and pick a capacity-growth scenario.
  4. Enter your farm's annual utilities spend in "Your farm's bill" for your own dollar figure.
  5. Explore the national map: territories shaded by the price effect, dots are data centers.

What the numbers mean

"Faster ag-price growth" — how much faster agricultural electricity prices have grown where more data-center capacity operates. Dollar figures estimate the added cost to a typical farm. Projections are scenarios, not forecasts.

🌾 Land

Data Centers & Farmland. What each data center was built on, how much farmland they consume, and whether they favor cropland.

How to use it

  1. Each dot is a data center, colored by what was there before: former farmland, already developed, other.
  2. Click a dot for operator, size, and what it replaced.
  3. Filter by state with the dropdown.
  4. Use the layer control (top-right): turn on County cropland share (green = where farmland is) and Building footprints — switch the basemap to Satellite and zoom in to see real outlines.

What the numbers mean

"26% on former farmland" — share of data centers built on cropland or pasture. "1.4× cropland targeting" — they sit on cropland about 1.4× more often than the surrounding area has, i.e. they favor flat, well-drained farmland.

💧 Water

Data Centers & Water. How data centers compete with farms for water — both their cooling-water use and the higher pumping costs that rising electricity prices bring.

How to use it

  1. Click any county on the map to load its water profile.
  2. Use "Add a data center here" (set new capacity in MW and your acres) to compare a facility's cooling-water demand to the county's farm groundwater use.
  3. Use the irrigation pumping-cost calculator — enter your pumping depth, water applied, system, and electricity rate — to see your pumping cost and how a price rise changes it.

What the numbers mean

Pumping cost — what it costs to lift irrigation water; deeper water means more energy and higher cost, so electricity-price rises hit deep-well irrigators hardest. The cooling-water bar compares a new data center's water use to local farm groundwater use.
Open Water →

Reading the maps

  • Dots are individual data centers; shaded areas are counties or utility territories.
  • Click anything — a dot, a county, a territory — to see its details.
  • Toggle map layers with the control in the map's corner; switch basemaps the same way.
  • Numbers labeled "scenario" show a possible future, not a prediction.
Preliminary research, for discussion and outreach — not official figures. Built from public data (USDA, EIA, USGS, NREL, NLCD/CDL, OpenStreetMap). One tool per resource; pick one above to begin.
